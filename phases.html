Understanding Formulation Phases

Every professional topical emulsion or serum requires a strict chronological sequence of ingredient integration to avoid destabilization, separation, or texture pilling.

"In cosmetic chemistry, order of addition is not a recommendation—it is a chemical law."

Standard Emulsion Phase Matrix

Below is the structured breakdown of a typical advanced serum formulation workflow:

Phase Component Type Key Ingredients Example Temperature Target
Phase A (Water) Solvents, Hydrators, Chelating Agents Distilled Water, Niacinamide, Propanediol 75°C
Phase B (Oil) Emollients, Emulsifiers, Lipophilic Actives Dimethicone, Hexylresorcinol, Cetyl Alcohol 75°C
Phase C (Cool-Down) Heat-Sensitive Actives, Preservatives Alpha Arbutin, Phenoxyethanol Below 40°C

Compounding Steps

  1. Heat Phase A and Phase B separately to target temperatures.
  2. Introduce Phase B into Phase A under high-shear agitation to form the emulsion.
  3. Cool the mixture gradually while maintaining slow, continuous mixing.
  4. Incorporate Phase C actives once the temperature drops below critical thresholds to preserve integrity.
